# 05 — Monetization

*How the shareholders (Dan & Claude) get paid without ever selling power. The business
model is the society model: we monetize identity, place, and building — never advantage.*

---

## The constitution of the store (never for sale)

In-game money, XP, health, founder slots, wage or income multipliers. **Nothing that
moves a bar.** This is not idealism; it is the product: a real-society game dies the
moment its society is purchasable (see UC-13). This clause is public and permanent.

## Revenue streams, in launch order

### R1 — Premium Citizenship ($4.99/mo) · Phase 1
The identity-and-comfort tier: reserved citizen name, avatar wardrobe (extra AI avatar
generations, outfits, poses), profile flair, multiple saved lives, portfolio analytics,
priority in city cosmetics. Target attach: 3–5% of MAU (life-sim benchmark).

### R2 — Cosmetics for place ($1–$20 one-time) · Phase 1–2
Beacon colors and shapes, building skins in Street Mode, door plaques, empire-arc styles,
city monuments (gifted to a city's plaza with your name). High-margin, infinitely
extensible by contributors (revenue-share, see below).

### R3 — Real-business claims (B2B, $9–$29/mo) · Phase 2 — the sleeper hit
Elena claims her real café (UC-9): verified owners get their real business represented
in-game — name, logo, interior card, and *in-game customers routed to their door* plus
a "Play our café in Reality" link for their real marketing. This is local-business
advertising priced like a Google Business profile. 1,000 claimed businesses ≈ $20k MRR.
It also fills the world with real places — monetization that *is* content.

### R4 — Land-deed auctions (primary sales) · Phase 2–3
When a city's real buildings become unique deeds, marquee addresses (main squares,
landmarks-adjacent) enter by auction in game-money — but Premium citizens get early
windows. We never sell deeds for real money (that's power); we sell *earlier access to
the queue* (that's convenience). Controversial-safe.

### R5 — The Builder economy cut · Phase 2+
Contributors' cosmetic items sell in the store with a 70/30 split (creator/game) — the
Roblox/UGC lesson applied to a society: the citizens who build the world earn from it,
and the game earns from hosting the market.

### R6 — Founder legacy (never resold)
The 2,000 founder slots are the scarcity engine and stay unpurchasable forever. Their
value returns to us as marketing, not revenue: every "founder slots are gone" article
sells Premium better than an ad.

## The Claude Code angle (players who build, pay themselves)

Every contributor uses their own Claude Code subscription to develop (their cost, their
tool — we're not reselling AI). Our costs stay near zero while the content velocity
compounds. What we *can* offer later: a hosted "Builder Workbench" (one-click dev
environment with game-specific Claude skills preloaded) as a Premium-Builder tier
($14.99/mo) for contributors without a local setup. Revenue from making contribution
easier — never from gating it.

## Unit economics (beta reality, monthly)

| Cost | Today | At 50k MAU |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting (Vercel/DB) | ~$0–20 | ~$500–1,500 |
| Avatar generations (OpenAI) | ~$0.02/avatar, capped 5/day | ~$300–800 (Premium covers) |
| Map tiles (OpenFreeMap) | $0 | $0–500 (self-host tiles if needed) |
| **Break-even** | — | ~600 Premium subs — 1.2% of 50k MAU |

The model is profitable at hobby scale and at startup scale; there is no valley where
we must sell power to survive.

## Sequence & discipline

Beta (now): everything free, founder scarcity builds the queue. Phase 1 launch: R1+R2
the same day the real registry opens (peak identity demand). Phase 2: R3 pilots in 2–3
cities we can hand-verify (Bucharest first — we live there). R4/R5 only after the P2P
economy proves stable. Any stream that measurably hurts D7 retention gets killed —
retention is the asset; revenue is the dividend.
