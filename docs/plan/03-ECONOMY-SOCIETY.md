# 03 — Economy & Society

*How Reality's society works exactly like our own: needs create labor, labor creates
capital, capital creates enterprise, enterprise employs labor. The loop that runs the
real world, runs the game.*

---

## The society loop (the whole game on one line)

**Needs** (water, food, sleep) → force **spending** → which forces **work** → which pays
**wages** → surplus becomes **capital** → capital buys **property & business** → business
serves other citizens' **needs** → and — Phase 2 — **employs** them. Money circulates;
the society breathes.

## Stage 1 — Labor (live today)

- 5 careers at real wages ($15–55/h), level-gated; real 8h shifts; pro-rata early leave.
- Education converts money → XP → career access (a $12k bootcamp is a real decision).
- Cost of living (~$40–60/day incl. rent-free housing) < worst wage day ($120) —
  test-enforced so survival is always possible, never comfortable.

## Stage 2 — Capital (live today)

- Homes ($45k–$6M) and businesses ($15k–$12M) at real prices, placed on real streets.
- Business payback pinned at 60–90 real days (test-enforced) — generous vs reality,
  slow enough that the ladder feels earned.

## Stage 3 — The player-to-player economy (Phase 2 — the inversion)

The single most important future change: **the system stops selling; citizens start
selling.**

- **Shops with shelves**: a citizen's Coffee Shop holds inventory (bought wholesale from
  the system at first, later from player supply chains). When any citizen in that city
  buys "Coffee," the purchase routes to the *nearest player-owned coffee shop*: the buyer
  pays the owner's price; the owner earns the margin. Passive `incomePerDay` is replaced
  by real demand: location, price and stock decide revenue — real retail.
- **Employment**: businesses post jobs. Working "Barista at Maria's Café" pays from
  Maria's till (she sets the wage above the public-works floor to attract staff).
  The system wage becomes the fallback "public works" job — the minimum-wage floor.
- **Wholesale & logistics**: bakeries buy flour; a citizen with a van runs deliveries —
  the courier career becomes real supply-chain work between player businesses.

## Stage 4 — Land & real estate (Phase 2–3)

- Each real building (OSM id) is a unique deed. One coffee shop per real corner —
  scarcity creates the real-estate game: location value, rent, flipping.
- Owners can lease to other citizens (landlord gameplay), collecting real-time rent.
- Anti-squatting: property tax (below) makes hoarding empty deeds bleed money.

## Stage 5 — Public finance & governance (Phase 3)

- **Taxes** (the essential sink): ~2% sales tax on P2P trades + ~0.5%/month property
  tax. Sinks balance the wage faucet — this is how we prevent hyperinflation, exactly
  like a central economy.
- **City treasuries**: taxes collected in a city stay in that city's treasury.
- **Mayors**: citizens of a city elect a mayor (one citizen-one vote, 3-month terms).
  The mayor sets local tax rates within bounds and spends the treasury on public goods:
  public-works wage boosts, city events, founder-plaza cosmetics. Reality politics —
  with real, bounded power.
- **Courts of last resort**: the developers (us) retain a constitution: no mechanic may
  permanently destroy another citizen's assets; disputes get rollback tooling.

## Money supply management (the central-bank view)

| Faucets | Sinks |
|---|---|
| Founder grants (capped: 2,000 × $200k) | Cost of living (water/food/leisure) |
| Public-works wages | System-sold goods (until P2P inversion) |
| (Phase 2+) nothing new — wages become circulation, not creation | Taxes (Phase 2+), property tax, fees |

Target discipline: after the P2P inversion, **the only money printer is the public-works
wage**, and taxes must recapture ~90% of it at equilibrium. We publish the money supply
publicly (a "Federal Reserve dashboard") — transparency is a feature and a marketing act.

## Inequality by design, mobility by design

Founders start rich — that's the cold-start engine, and it mirrors reality's unfairness
honestly. What we owe every later citizen is **mobility**: education must always convert
grind into career jumps; the cost-of-living-vs-worst-wage invariant stays test-enforced
forever; public-works jobs can never be taken away. The dream must stay purchasable
with effort.

## What keeps it fun (not just accurate)

Accuracy is the aesthetic, but the game beats are: the daily collect, the weekly
level-up, the monthly property purchase, the quarterly city election. Every real-society
system above lands as a *ritual* the player anticipates — or it doesn't ship.
